About QuantumVault

QuantumVault is free and open-source software, released under the GNU Affero General Public License v3.0 (AGPLv3), that lets anyone run their own zero-knowledge, zero-trust, end-to-end encrypted messaging and file vault using hybrid post-quantum cryptography.

What QuantumVault Does

QuantumVault combines a post-quantum key encapsulation mechanism (ML-KEM-768, FIPS 203) with classical X25519 elliptic-curve key exchange, derives keys via HKDF-SHA256, and seals data with AES-256-GCM. Every cryptographic operation that matters — key generation, encryption, decryption — happens in your browser, using vendored, audit-friendly cryptographic libraries. The platform lets you:

  • Encrypt and store files: files are encrypted client-side before they ever leave your device, then stored as opaque ciphertext on the server you connect to.
  • Exchange end-to-end encrypted messages: messages are sealed with a hybrid post-quantum key exchange so that only the sender and the intended recipient can ever read them.
  • Authenticate without ever sending your password: login uses SRP-6a, a zero-knowledge password proof — your password never crosses the network in any form.
  • Resist quantum and classical attacks alike: the hybrid construction means an attacker would need to break both ML-KEM-768 and X25519 to recover your keys, today or with a future quantum computer.

Zero-Knowledge, Zero-Trust by Design

"Zero-knowledge" means the server storing your data never has access to your plaintext, your private keys, or your password — not because of a policy promise, but because the cryptography makes it structurally impossible. "Zero-trust" means you do not need to trust the operator of a QuantumVault instance to keep that promise: you can read the source code yourself, verify the cryptographic primitives in use, and confirm that the protections described here are real.

The architecture is intentionally hybrid: communications are relayed and encrypted blobs are stored through a central server for convenience and reachability, but that server is reduced to a transport and storage layer. It never holds the keys needed to read what passes through it.

Why LazyOwn RedTeam Released This as Free Software

QuantumVault was built by LazyOwn RedTeam, a security research and offensive security collective. We spend our time finding the gaps that surveillance, censorship, and authoritarian control exploit — and we know how often ordinary people, journalists, activists, and dissidents are left without tools they can actually trust.

Rather than keep this technology behind a paywall or a closed SaaS, we are releasing QuantumVault to the public as AGPLv3 free software. Anyone — an independent newsroom, an NGO, a community group, or an individual — can deploy their own instance, inspect every line of code, modify it, and run it under their own infrastructure and their own responsibility. Our goal is to make robust, post-quantum end-to-end encrypted communication available as a public good, in defense of free expression and a free press against any form of tyranny or censorship.

Free Software, Self-Hosted

Because QuantumVault is AGPLv3, anyone running a modified version of it as a network service must make the corresponding source code available to its users. This keeps every deployment auditable. Each instance of QuantumVault is operated independently by whoever deployed it; LazyOwn RedTeam provides the software, not a hosted service, and does not control or have visibility into independently operated instances.

QuantumVault: free software, post-quantum encryption, for a free press and free expression.
